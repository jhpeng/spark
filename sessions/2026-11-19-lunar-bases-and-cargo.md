# Session: Lunar bases, early resource use, and Earth-return cargo

## Ideas discussed

- Early resource priorities — reworked into separate local uses: bulk regolith for earthworks, water if suitable deposits exist, and oxygen from minerals. Lunar resources do not imply a producing mine or a profitable Earth export. The illustrative water mass balances pass arithmetic checks; resource quality and complete plant engineering remain unresolved.
- First base location — the Moon is a candidate for a first off-Earth surface industrial outpost, not the first human orbital workplace or an inevitable destination for all industry. Polar resources, lighting, terrain, and logistics must be assessed together; working and living areas need not coincide.
- NASA's plans — reviewed primary NASA sources as of 2026-11-19. Recorded the phased approach, early contracts versus future capabilities, and the 2026 architecture changes as dated references only. No NASA target was promoted into Spark's timeline or treated as an already operating base.
- Motive-specific development — applied all eight existing motive branches to a conditional outpost. Preserved government services, optional AI/orbital supply, development-limit industry, indirect climate-adaptation uses, community residence, civilization backup, the separately scoped military hypothesis, and limited Earth-centered development. A large industry need not entail many permanent residents.
- Local production versus imports — the assumed 30-tonne initial import, 10-tonne annual substitution, and 2-tonne annual plant imports give 3.75 years to offset imported mass after steady operation begins. Reworked to distinguish mass accounting from financial payback and equipment feasibility.
- User's transport-first proposal — preserved as `lunar-earth-export`: develop small returns and pilot production together, validate delivery to Earth's surface, then expand export plants and transport against credible demand. This does not require completing a giant launcher before any production, or assume a lunar product is already competitive.
- Return difficulty — reworked from “may not be too difficult” into demonstrated physical return but unproven routine low-cost freight. Departure, transfer, and reentry/recovery are distinct cost centers. Apollo's approximately 382 kg of returned samples establishes return, not industrial economics.
- Electromagnetic departure — retained as a candidate alongside rockets. A surface foundation transfers recoil to the Moon; this differs from the existing free-flying accelerator draft and does not retcon it. Prototype evidence supports the mechanism, not a full industrial launcher.
- Energy checks — lunar escape-speed reference gives about 2.83 MJ/kg or 0.79 kWh/kg; an approximately 11 km/s Earth entry gives 60.5 MJ/kg or 16.8 kWh/kg. Arithmetic is consistent, but neither is an end-to-end trajectory, power bill, freight price, or heat-shield design. No new physics is required; complete engineering and adoption remain unsettled.

## Decisions

- The user requested that useful inferences from this discussion be written into the repository. This authorizes draft recording, not canon promotion or approval of a unique future.
- Create three bilingual concept entries, add their scope to the expansion-motives entry, and synchronize unresolved checks in both open-question files.
- Keep existing motive identifiers. Define `lunar-local-supply` and `lunar-earth-export` with explicit assumptions and dependencies; no automatic inheritance of all motives, mature infrastructure, or the military hypothesis.
- Keep scientific return, local industrial supply, orbital supply, Earth-surface exports, permanent residence, and independent survival distinct.
- Preserve NASA source dates and plan status. Leave canon laws and timeline unchanged.

## Open questions raised

- Which actual site, product, process, and recurring customer justify local lunar production, including energy, thermal control, maintenance, and site logistics?
- Which shared services have sufficient utilization and funding duration, and what survives sponsor withdrawal?
- Which Earth customer and accepted product volume support the entire production-and-return chain?
- Which departure, transfer, reentry, recovery, and hardware reuse arrangements meet the intended cargo service?
- At what throughput and operating life does a fixed electromagnetic launcher outperform rocket freight?
- What health, rights, supply, and defined Earth-support outage duration would make a lunar residential or backup extension viable?
