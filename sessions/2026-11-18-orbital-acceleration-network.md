# Session: Orbital acceleration network

## Ideas discussed

- The user supplied a recap of an earlier discussion spanning electric, chemical, and nuclear propulsion; photon and particle exhaust; fusion confinement; ambient propellant collection; and resource networks. This is background provenance, not independent validation or canon approval of every claim in that recap. No particular fusion drive, collector, or nuclear power source was selected.
- Reusable orbital electromagnetic acceleration stations — passed conservation-law screening; detailed engineering and economic feasibility remain open. Stations receive recoil and must restore their operating orbits. Jupiter is a candidate location.
- Passenger and cargo service using adjusted output power — reworked to require controlled force and timing as well as adequate interaction distance. Lower acceleration takes longer to supply the same velocity change.
- A station made of separate modules, each adding a small velocity increment — passed conservation-law screening. The user specified a formation based on incoming trajectory and required acceleration; the agent's earlier single-structure assumption was superseded during discussion.
- Sparse formations — qualified: coasting gaps add no acceleration, brief small boosts may still impose large loads, and each module receives its own recoil.
- Shared infrastructure as a transport business — motive identified, but costs, traffic, resource supply, and comparison with alternatives remain unsettled.

- Use separate accelerator modules that arrange encounters around the planned ship trajectory and required boost.
- Preserve the distinction between design intentions and illustrative calculations. The 100-tonne ship, 1 g and 100 g profiles, 1 km interaction range, and 90% efficiency are assumptions for analysis, not selected hardware specifications or established tolerances.
