# Forecast tree rules

Use `forecasts/` for evidence-based predictions and `forecasts-tw/` for their translations. Read [canon rules](../canon/AGENTS.md) for physical validation, [session rules](../sessions/AGENTS.md) for recording, and [format rules](../templates/AGENTS.md) for language and links.

1. **Global baseline and directory.** `index.md` is the root entry: record the dated real-world baseline (evidence cutoff, observed conditions, dated sources, unknowns), shared reading and resolution conventions, and an index of active forecast trees. The preceding five to ten years may inform the baseline.
2. **One tree per topic file.** Keep each thematic prediction tree in a single file (`forecasts/<topic>.md`) using [templates/forecast.md](../templates/forecast.md) (and `templates/forecast-tw.md` for `forecasts-tw/`). Split a deep subtree into a child file (`forecasts/<topic>/<subtree>.md`) only when a file becomes unwieldy (roughly over 350–400 lines) or evolves into an independent theme. Every outcome node still uses a stable lowercase-kebab ID, one parent (baseline or prior outcome ID), and explicit conditional and cumulative path probabilities. Keep the tree connected and acyclic; each outcome inherits its entire ancestral scenario and adds its own conditions and consequences. Similar outcomes under different histories remain separate nodes with distinct IDs.
3. **One question per fork.** Order forks chronologically or top-down within the tree file. State the inherited path, question, deadline, observable outcome criteria, and enabling or blocking conditions. Define shared milestone criteria and resolution rules once in the tree header rather than repeating boilerplate in every fork. Sibling outcomes must be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive; include an explicit residual outcome when needed. Events that can coexist require successive forks or joint outcomes. Actively extend promising leaves into second- and third-order downstream forks — including counterintuitive risks, bottlenecks, and long-horizon `speculative` branches — whenever physics and causal logic make them informative, rather than padding trivial branches.
4. **Conditional and path probabilities.** Every outcome gives its conditional probability given its full ancestral scenario and current evidence, plus its cumulative path probability (the product of conditional probabilities along the path; this does not assume independence). Active siblings sum to 100% for the same question, deadline, and evidence cutoff. Include a top-level tree overview (indented tree or Mermaid diagram) showing conditional probability, cumulative path probability, and resolution status. Recalculate affected siblings and descendants together.
5. **Evidence and confidence.** Separate real-world observations, user premises, and agent extrapolations or deductions. Cite dated sources and explain each estimate, including subjective judgments when data are scarce. Record confidence separately from probability; use coarse estimates and broader outcomes for distant horizons. Scenario assumptions from wiki, canon, or agent extrapolations are not observations of the real world.
6. **Autonomous extension and revision.** Freely create new thematic forecast trees and extend or update existing ones from real-world evidence, discussion, or your own forward extrapolations — without human approval. Do not wait for the user to propose a branch before modeling its downstream consequences, competing mechanisms, or tail risks; trees, forks, and outcomes may be added, revised, split, or retired autonomously. Retain prior dated baselines, estimates, criteria, and reasons for material changes in the file's revision history, the session log, or linked repository history. Repair links and translations. Keep retired outcomes recorded in the tree's revision history (outside active probability sums) and move retired standalone trees to `forecasts/archive/`; represent any still-possible outcomes in active alternatives.
7. **Resolution and convergence.** Use `resolution: pending | occurred | not-occurred | unresolved`; use `lifecycle: active | retired` separately. Resolve outcomes only against their stated criteria and deadlines; missing evidence remains unresolved. Advance the baseline and recondition surviving forecasts as events become known, preserving the old forecast and its evaluation. New evidence may widen uncertainty or introduce branches; convergence is not mandatory.
8. **Scope and authority.** Forecasts follow known physics and declare the scope of borrowed worldbuilding premises. These forecast lifecycle rules govern forecast files instead of the canon approval statuses. Forecast revisions neither promote content to canon nor change approved claims or the canon timeline; those changes follow the canon guide.

## Minimum tree record

- Frontmatter: `title`, `id`, `period`, `evidence_cutoff`, `updated`, `lifecycle`.
- Scope, inherited baseline link, and tree overview (showing conditional probability, cumulative path probability, and `resolution`).
- Shared observable milestone criteria and resolution rules.
- Per fork: inherited path, question, deadline, evidence cutoff; outcomes table (`id`, outcome, conditional probability, cumulative path probability, confidence, `resolution`, `lifecycle`); concise rationale and validation/revision signals; explicit dependencies.
- Checkpoints/revision triggers (when useful), unresolved validation links, and revision history with session provenance.

Before completing an update, check tree connectivity, inherited assumptions, sibling probability totals (100%), cumulative path products, affected descendants, revision provenance, inbound links, and bilingual synchronization.
