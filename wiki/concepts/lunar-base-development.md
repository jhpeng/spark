# Conditional development of lunar bases

## Summary

A lunar research outpost could become a shared service site, an industrial supplier, a residential community, or a civilization-backup project under different motives and commitments. NASA's published plans provide a dated real-world reference, not guaranteed Spark events. Useful infrastructure can persist without extensive industry or permanent families.

## Reasoning

### Conditional starting point

For the extensions below, assume an exploration program successfully establishes limited surface power, communications, cargo delivery, vehicles, and accommodation for rotating crews. This is an exploratory premise, not existing infrastructure or shared canon. It does not include a mature mine, independent food supply, mass immigration, or the proposed orbital accelerator.

Each row adopts only that starting point and the assumptions of its named branch, as defined in [wiki/concepts/space-expansion-motives](./space-expansion-motives.md) and the linked entries. No row automatically inherits another. The common question is what recurring, funded activity would pay for an additional facility.

A possible combination explicitly adopts `state-funded-expansion` to support initial services, then adds `ai-industrial-expansion` and/or `earth-development-limits` only if their distinct demand and supply conditions hold. It could progress from research to shared logistics and repair, local consumables, and deliveries to external customers. Neither the combination nor this order is required by the other branches. If local production is selected, additionally adopt the assumptions of `lunar-local-supply` in [wiki/tech/lunar-resource-utilization](../tech/lunar-resource-utilization.md). Earth-surface exports instead require the separate case in [wiki/concepts/lunar-earth-cargo](./lunar-earth-cargo.md).

Community organization and autonomy are described in [wiki/society/community-led-settlement](../society/community-led-settlement.md); employer-supported migration, residence rights, and exit arrangements are examined in [wiki/society/migration-contracts-and-resident-rights](../society/migration-contracts-and-resident-rights.md). These institutional options are not automatically established by industrial infrastructure.

For the optional geopolitical motive within `state-funded-expansion`, [wiki/concepts/geopolitical-drivers-of-space-expansion](./geopolitical-drivers-of-space-expansion.md) explains how governments may fund independent access before a broad market exists. Its dated Chinese lunar-station reference explicitly envisages autonomous operation with short human visits. Such competition can support the shared-service path above without choosing the residential path. Any later user still needs access to the facilities, a funded purpose, and a plan for continuity if the initial sponsor withdraws.

## Validation

### NASA reference, checked 2026-11-19

These are published goals and development activities, not approved future events or promises that every element will be built.

| Published phase | Intended capability |
|---|---|
| Through 2029 | Reliable access, robotic reconnaissance, science, vehicles, and technology demonstrations. |
| 2029–2032 | Initial power, communications, logistics, and mobility infrastructure across suitable sites. |
| From 2032 | Assemble and expand facilities for longer human stays. This is the start of a phase, not a completion date. |

NASA describes a phased buildup near the south pole. Its systems outline includes early short-stay pressurized accommodation followed by larger habitats, airlocks, and connecting nodes. A Japanese pressurized rover is intended as mobile accommodation and a laboratory. These goals do not demonstrate uninterrupted occupancy or self-sufficiency. [NASA: phases](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/), [NASA: systems](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-systems/), [NASA: pressurized rover](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase/pressurized-rover/)

The November 7, 2026 update distinguishes early contracts from completed infrastructure: miniCAST power/communications work starts with components and integration, while a separate integrated-power solicitation is anticipated. PROMISE is a planned reconnaissance rover, with a late-2028 launch target and an 18-month mission, not a producing mine. [NASA: November update](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-moon-base-transportation-technology-science-projects/)

The 2026 architecture redefined Artemis III as an Earth-orbit test and targeted a 2028 crewed lunar return; NASA announced its intention to pause Gateway in its then-current form and emphasize surface infrastructure. This reference must not silently inherit older mission sequencing. [NASA: March architecture update](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-unveils-initiatives-to-achieve-americas-national-space-policy/), [NASA: October lander update](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/)

Oxygen extraction and manufacturing appear in technology solicitations, not as guaranteed early production services. [NASA: lunar technology solicitation](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-calls-for-proposals-to-accelerate-lunar-surface-technologies/)

### Illustrative imported-mass comparison

Assume a local plant requires **30 tonnes** of imported equipment, including added power, heat rejection, and initial spares. At steady operation it replaces **10 tonnes/year** of deliveries to the same surface site while needing **2 tonnes/year** of imported consumables and spares:

**t = 30 / (10 − 2) = 3.75 years.**

This is the time to offset additional imported mass after steady production starts, not financial payback or calendar time from investment. It assumes all output is used, unchanged demand and performance, and adequate equipment life. It excludes startup time and does not establish capital, operating, financing, failure, or transport-price costs. A short mission may not use enough output; a long contract or an explicitly funded resilience benefit may strengthen the case. These are hypothetical quantities, not a plant design.

### Consistency and remaining physical checks

Checked [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md), [canon/timeline](../../canon/timeline.md), the motive branches, and related resource/transport entries during [sessions/2026-11-19-lunar-bases-and-cargo](../../sessions/2026-11-19-lunar-bases-and-cargo.md). There is no conflicting approved expansion date, site, or industrial sequence. All motive entries remain drafts. No timeline change or canon promotion is made.

No complete plant power, thermal, radiation, lifetime, food, or transport budget has been demonstrated. Specific expansions must close those budgets; paid demand and existing service equipment do not remove conservation, health, or maintenance limits. A working industrial site does not establish a family settlement or an independently surviving civilization.

## Consequences

- The same initial outpost can grow toward different facilities because customers value different results.
- Shared services and uncrewed remote nodes can precede large factories or a second inhabited base.
- Facilities, industrial output, permanent residents, and autonomous survival can grow at different rates.
- Terrestrial export logistics can be developed alongside pilot production rather than after a large export factory already exists.

## Open questions

- Which recurring contracts and utilization rates justify each shared service, and what remains viable if the first sponsor leaves?
- What full costs and equipment life support local production beyond the imported-mass illustration?
- For settlement and backup extensions, what health capabilities, rights, supply arrangements, and outage duration define success?

These checks are tracked in [open-questions](../../open-questions.md); the alternative branches remain here rather than being treated as questions that must have one winner.
