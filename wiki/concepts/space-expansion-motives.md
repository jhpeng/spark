# Motives for space expansion

## Summary

Exploration, commercial operations, permanent settlement, and a self-sustaining civilization need different explanations. This entry compares conditional motives for leaving Earth without assuming resource exhaustion, inevitable migration, or one universal future.

## Reasoning

### Shared scope

Across the branches compared here, distinguish the actor, the payer, the customer or beneficiary, and the conditions for persistence. Physical feasibility does not establish profitability, and profitable space infrastructure does not establish a reason for families to move there. Only known physical laws are binding shared canon; the analytical distinctions and scenarios here remain draft.

American westward migration provides examples of economic opportunity, hardship, persecution, and organized support acting together. It does not provide a universal sequence to copy into space. A desire to leave can coexist with inability to pay for relocation; access to assets and social mobility can matter even when resources have not physically run out.

### Corporate and household decisions

A company can put equipment in space while keeping its customers, headquarters, and most staff on Earth. Candidate motives include providing a service that benefits from an orbital location; fulfilling government contracts; supplying existing space customers locally; exploiting an advantageous production environment; and investing early in capabilities that may attract later customers. Each needs a distinct cost and demand case. Supplier chains need a final paying use, rather than a mine and transport company each justifying the other through circular demand.

Households may instead seek income, housing, family prospects, cultural autonomy, or safety. [wiki/society/migration-contracts-and-resident-rights](../society/migration-contracts-and-resident-rights.md) describes the optional employer-supported route and its dependence on enforceable promises, net income, and viable exit. [wiki/society/community-led-settlement](../society/community-led-settlement.md) explores collective migration for cultural or institutional autonomy. Neither arrangement is a universal condition of expansion.

Moving from a work site to a settlement requires an additional case for long residence, healthcare, education, and family life. Reliable automation can leave a successful space industry with few residents. Orbital, lunar, and Martian locations require separate comparisons; distance alone neither creates autonomy nor proves commercial value.

### State competition and household migration

[wiki/concepts/geopolitical-drivers-of-space-expansion](./geopolitical-drivers-of-space-expansion.md) examines how independent access and international influence can motivate early spending within `state-funded-expansion`. State-funded services may make later activities more affordable without giving households a reason to move. A government can value a predominantly robotic site or rotating crew; residents still need their own income, health, family, and rights case. This optional motive does not require resource exhaustion or global development limits, and it does not make permanent settlement an automatic consequence of competition.

### Branch: `civilization-backup`

- **Grounding:** `speculative`; survival after prolonged loss of Earth support is not demonstrated by current space habitation.
- **Assumptions:** States, donors, or communities fund long-term resilience even without ordinary commercial returns, and eventually establish sufficient independent food, energy, maintenance, and manufacturing capability.
- **Divergence:** The payer values continuity of civilization beyond Earth, rather than only immediate services or settlement preferences.
- **Consequences:** A small, expensive settlement could precede mass migration. A permanently inhabited but import-dependent base does not yet meet the backup objective.
- **Dependencies:** [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md) and an explicit inventory of supplies, skills, and industrial capabilities that remain available after Earth support is interrupted; no specific propulsion technology is assumed.

### Branch: `earth-centered-development`

- **Grounding:** `plausible`; it extends terrestrial development and limited space operations without requiring a new self-sufficient off-world industrial system.
- **Assumptions:** Efficiency, recycling, substitution, adaptation, and terrestrial production remain adequate or cheaper for most paying needs, or finance and demand cannot support major off-world expansion.
- **Divergence:** There is no sustained transition to large-scale off-world industry and settlement. Satellite services and limited research missions can still continue.
- **Consequences:** Successful AI or environmental adaptation can coexist with most industry and population remaining on Earth. This is a viable outcome, not a failed validation to remove.
- **Dependencies:** [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md) and the branch's terrestrial supply assumptions; it does not inherit comprehensive development bans or irreversible environmental collapse.

## Validation

### Historical analogy and its limits

The following are historical reference points, not future premises:

| Example | Motive or enabling condition |
|---|---|
| Oregon migration | Economic depressions in 1837 and 1841 encouraged a search for opportunity; fertile-land claims and political promotion also attracted migrants. [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/oregon-trail-junior-ranger.htm) |
| California gold rush | The prospect of wealth attracted people from existing occupations and many regions; hardship alone does not explain participation. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/california-first-person-narratives/articles-and-essays/early-california-history/discovery-of-gold/) |
| Homesteading | The 1862 Act offered qualifying applicants a route to 160 acres, subject to requirements including residence and improvement. It was not unrestricted ownership by arrival. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/homestead-act) |
| Mormon migration | Persecution and the desire for a religious community motivated organized migration. [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/pisp/planyourvisit/mormon-story.htm) |
| Exodusters | Oppression and violence in the South combined with hopes for freedom and opportunity in Kansas. [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/exodusters.htm) |

NPS estimates that outfitting an emigrant journey and sustaining a year without a harvest cost roughly $800–$1,200 in 1850. Poverty could therefore obstruct departure as well as motivate it. Settler opportunities also involved dispossession of Indigenous peoples, so the analogy must not treat the West as vacant land. [NPS: travel costs](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/traveling-emigrant-trails.htm), [NPS: Oregon Trail guide](https://home.nps.gov/oreg/planyourvisit/upload/National-Park-Service-Large-Print-Trail-Brochure-508.pdf)

Space also lacks the readily usable atmosphere of a terrestrial destination. Existing law does not reproduce homestead-style sovereignty: Article II of the Outer Space Treaty bars national appropriation of celestial bodies through sovereignty claims, use, or occupation. Future residence, facility ownership, and resource-benefit arrangements require their own definitions. [United Nations: treaty text](https://www.unoosa.org/pdf/publications/ST_SPACE_51E.pdf)

### Physics: habitation is a continuing supply problem

NASA reported a 98% water-recovery milestone on the ISS and approximately one gallon per person per day for drinking, food preparation, and some hygiene. This demonstrates a particular system result, not a self-sufficient settlement. [NASA: water recovery](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/)

For an illustrative 1,000-person settlement, assume 4 kg/person/day enters a recoverable water loop and 98% is recovered. Makeup water is 1,000 × 4 × (1 − 0.98) = **80 kg/day**, or about **29 tonnes/year** over 365 days. This excludes agriculture, industry, additional leakage, maintenance consumables, and other losses. Makeup could be locally extracted rather than imported, but the extraction and processing system must then be provided. The estimate demonstrates a continuing requirement, not a complete life-support design.

Radiation, altered gravity, isolation, and closed environments remain additional constraints. No shielding mass, habitat lifetime, or multigenerational health capability is established here. [NASA: human-spaceflight hazards](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/)

## Consequences

- Commercial equipment can expand well before permanent population, and may never require mass settlement.
- Contracts, access to assets, and dependence on suppliers can shape migration as much as resource abundance.
- A destination must justify continued operation after its first sponsor, subsidy, or initial enthusiasm ends.
- Branches can combine commercial and cultural motives without making environmental collapse or resource exhaustion universal premises.

## Open questions

- Across settlement branches, what employment, finance, residence rights, and family services make long residence preferable to rotation or remaining on Earth?
- For `community-led-settlement` and `civilization-backup`, what support inventory and local capabilities establish the intended degree of autonomy, and for how long can Earth supply be interrupted?
- For all expansion branches, which destination and final paying use justify the first sustained operation, and what happens if demand or sponsorship ends?
