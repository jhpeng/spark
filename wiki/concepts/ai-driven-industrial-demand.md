# AI-driven industrial demand

## Summary

AI could lower the cost of design, research, coordination, and some physical operations, allowing more projects to reach production. This branch explores whether paying demand can grow faster than efficiency improves, making energy, materials, and manufacturing capacity scarce enough to support selected off-world investments; it does not assume unlimited demand or rapid exhaustion of Earth.

## Reasoning

### Branch: `ai-industrial-expansion`

- **Grounding:** `speculative`; widespread reliable physical automation and economically competitive large-scale off-world supply remain unproven dependencies.
- **Assumptions:** AI produces useful, affordable improvements; customers or public purchasers can pay for more services and physical outcomes; production and robotics become reliable enough for the proposed tasks. For sustained resource growth, additional activity exceeds savings from efficiency, sharing, substitution, and recycling.
- **Divergence:** Productive plans and orders accumulate faster than some energy, equipment, or material supply chains can expand. Firms invest in supply assurance and may finance off-world capacity where it beats available alternatives.
- **Consequences:** Long-term power and manufacturing contracts become valuable; some firms integrate into upstream supply. Robotics can lower the labor burden of space operations, while also leaving successful industries largely uninhabited. Price increases, demand saturation, technical failure, or better terrestrial alternatives can interrupt expansion.
- **Dependencies:** [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md), specified paying applications, credible automation, and validated energy/material/transport budgets. [wiki/tech/orbital-data-centers](../tech/orbital-data-centers.md) is one optional pathway. Neither [wiki/concepts/earth-development-limits](./earth-development-limits.md) nor [wiki/concepts/climate-adaptation-pressure](./climate-adaptation-pressure.md) is inherited automatically; the earlier orbital accelerator is not required.

The alternative `earth-centered-development`, defined in [wiki/concepts/space-expansion-motives](./space-expansion-motives.md), retains strong AI with predominantly terrestrial industry when those conditions are not met. It is a viable future rather than a question requiring a single choice now.

### Where final demand could come from

Supply capability alone does not create purchases. These are candidate applications, not forecasts of universal adoption or proven markets at a stated scale:

| Desired outcome | Candidate product or service | Physical demand and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Less housework and greater independence | Cleaning, meal preparation, suitable assistance, repair coordination | Robots or dedicated appliances, energy, maintenance, and some computing; household tasks are finite, shared services can suffice, and safety/reliability must be established. A humanoid robot per person is not assumed. |
| More comfortable housing | Better insulation, cooling, indoor air quality, reliable water, and maintenance | Buildings, equipment, utilities, and materials; household budgets, land, population, and efficient design constrain growth. |
| Richer learning and entertainment | Personalized tutoring and persistent interactive worlds | Computing, communications, and terminals; attention and time are finite, perceived quality can saturate, and digital services may replace more material-intensive activities. |
| Completing personal or community projects | Customized products, building renovations, gardens, creative production | Manufacturing, construction, logistics, and materials; a cheap design does not finance land or construction. |
| More research and productive activity | Automated experiments, prototype exploration, engineering services | Laboratory equipment, trials, factories, and energy; downstream households, businesses, donors, or governments must ultimately fund useful outcomes. |

If AI income accrues narrowly while household purchasing power falls, many wishes remain unfunded. Lower prices, labor income, ownership income, or public provision could support broader access, but none is adopted as a universal distribution rule. Environmental adaptation can create another category of necessity in `climate-adaptation-pressure`; that is a separate branch, not evidence that discretionary demand is infinite.

### Capacity scarcity rather than assumed planetary exhaustion

Orders can exceed available output even when resources remain in the ground. Mining, refining, equipment production, grid connections, and semiconductor fabrication have different bottlenecks. Copper for conductors and power infrastructure, rare earths used in some permanent magnets, and silicon and specialized inputs for chips cannot be treated as one interchangeable category of scarce AI material.

A space mine does not directly solve shortages of advanced chips, manufacturing equipment, memory, packaging, specialized chemicals, or process expertise. A candidate early division of labor keeps complex electronics on Earth while considering off-world bulk structures or consumables for customers already in space. Even this requires evidence about extraction, refining, specifications, transport, and cost. Shipping materials back to Earth needs a separate economic case.

### A conditional route into space

One possible sequence is paying Earth demand → commercially viable orbital computing → repeat orders for power, radiators, structures, and servicing → selected off-world production. Earth customers provide the final use, so a mine and its transport supplier do not have to justify each other through circular demand.

AI could increase demand and reduce the cost of some autonomous construction and maintenance at the same time. It still does not remove energy, heat rejection, wear, material, finance, or construction-time constraints. Finite demand can support a long expansion period without an assumption of endless growth. Growth in Earth consumption alone does not prove a need for any particular space industry.

## Validation

### Illustrative demand, energy, and material balances

All numbers in these examples are assumptions for accounting, not demographic, engineering, or market forecasts.

- **Efficiency and activity:** If energy per completed task falls to 1/10 while the number of tasks rises 30-fold, task energy becomes 30 × 0.1 = **3 times** its original value. A smaller increase in task count could instead reduce energy. This is a conditional rebound mechanism, not proof that it will occur. It excludes embodied energy and changes in the task mix.
- **Recurring services:** 100 million households purchasing 2 hours/day produce **200 million service-hours/day**. This measures an output people might value, not a required robot count or centralized compute load. Sharing, utilization, task capabilities, and price must be specified before converting hours into hardware demand.
- **Growing material stock:** With unchanged material use per unit of equipment, increasing metal embodied in operating equipment from 100 to 130 units requires **30 additional units**. Recycling retired stock can reduce primary material demand for replacements, but metal still operating in the original equipment cannot simultaneously become the expansion stock. The example does not assume unavoidable waste, fixed technology, or the absence of inventories.

Physical output is bounded by supplied energy and material, while operating equipment must reject waste heat under [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md). No deployment scale, launch mass, or end-to-end industrial energy budget is established. The optional orbital computing pathway has its own checks in [wiki/tech/orbital-data-centers](../tech/orbital-data-centers.md).

### Evidence and limits

IEA's 2026 update projects total data-centre electricity consumption rising from roughly 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, about 3% of global electricity demand then. These figures include non-AI data-centre uses and are projections, not evidence of inevitable planetary energy scarcity. The same report identifies supply bottlenecks, financing sensitivity, and efficiency opportunities. [IEA: Key Questions on Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

OECD describes specialized semiconductor production stages and limited interchangeability between fabrication plants; a general increase in mineral supply therefore cannot be equated with advanced-chip capacity. USGS distinguishes silicon's semiconductor role from rare earth applications including magnets and catalysts. [OECD: semiconductor supply chains](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/semiconductors.html), [OECD: fabrication capacity](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-chip-landscape_02dbd028-en/full-report/component-3.html), [USGS: critical mineral uses](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/mineral-resources-program/science/about-2025-list-critical-minerals)

IEA's minerals outlook and recycling assessment support separating supply-chain constraints, mining and refining expansion, and secondary supply. They do not establish that Earth will soon run out of minerals or that space extraction is competitive. [IEA: minerals outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026/executive-summary), [IEA: recycling](https://www.iea.org/reports/recycling-of-critical-minerals/executive-summary)

WHO estimates that over 2.5 billion people need one or more assistive products, including ordinary products such as glasses and hearing aids. This supports the existence of unmet assistance needs, not a market estimate for humanoid robots. IEA projects residential air-conditioner stock in Southeast Asia to triple by 2035, illustrating a concrete comfort demand without proving that all consumption categories will grow similarly. [WHO: assistive technology](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/assistive-technology), [IEA: Southeast Asia outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/southeast-asia-energy-outlook-2026/executive-summary)

### Consistency and motive

Checked [canon/laws](../../canon/laws.md), [canon/timeline](../../canon/timeline.md), and related repository terms. No approved AI adoption trajectory, resource shortage, or space-industry date conflicts with this conditional branch. [wiki/tech/orbital-transportation-network](../tech/orbital-transportation-network.md) remains a draft with unproven demand and is not assumed available. The discussion is recorded in [sessions/2026-11-19-drivers-of-space-expansion](../../sessions/2026-11-19-drivers-of-space-expansion.md).

Households, businesses, and public purchasers fund final outcomes; firms and investors finance capacity in expectation of those payments. Technical feasibility, household need, willingness to pay, and investor returns are separate claims. Efficiency, saturation, inequality, substitution, shared services, and terrestrial expansion can all weaken the demand case without violating physics.

## Consequences

- Firms may seek long-term power, materials, and manufacturing access before expanding customer services.
- Cheap design can coexist with expensive physical execution and long delivery queues.
- Better AI can support either more resource use or greater efficiency; the balance is conditional.
- Even a large off-world industry may require few permanent human residents; settlement remains a separate motive and feasibility problem in [wiki/concepts/space-expansion-motives](./space-expansion-motives.md).

## Open questions

- For `ai-industrial-expansion`, which final applications have sufficiently large paying demand, and how do prices, efficiency, attention limits, and substitution change it?
- What purchasing-power or public-funding mechanism turns household needs into sustained orders in this branch?
- Which energy, mineral, refining, chip, equipment, and labor bottlenecks actually bind at the selected scale, after recycling and terrestrial alternatives?
- What reliability, maintenance, and safety can physical AI deliver for the chosen tasks, especially in space?
- Which first off-world product or service has a complete cost and environmental advantage, including construction, transport, financing, replacements, and disposal?

Unresolved checks are tracked in [open-questions](../../open-questions.md).
